\begin{problem}{\bf Computing GCDs without long division} (10 points)\\
One of the drawbacks of the Euclidean algorithm is that it requires
long divisions which can be difficult to implement in software and
hardware. The following algorithm for computing greatest common divisors
only uses divisions by 2, an operation which can be easily implemented
as a right-shift of the binary representation.
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep 0mm
\item[] {\bf Algorithm: GCDNoDivision}
\item[] Input: Positive integers $a$, $b$.
\item[] Output: $\gcd(a,b)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep 0mm
\item[(i)] $e \leftarrow 1$.
\item[(ii)] While $a$ and $b$ are both even do\\
      \hspace*{10mm} $a \leftarrow a/2$,
                     $\;\;\;\;b \leftarrow b/2$,
                     $\;\;\;\;e \leftarrow 2e$.
\item[(iii)] While $a \neq 0$ do\\
      \hspace*{10mm}While $a$ is even do: $a \leftarrow a/2$.\\
      \hspace*{10mm}While $b$ is even do: $b \leftarrow b/2$.\\
      \hspace*{10mm}If $a \geq b$ then\\
      \hspace*{20mm}$a \leftarrow a-b$\\
      \hspace*{10mm}else\\
      \hspace*{20mm}$b \leftarrow b-a$
\item[(iv)] $d \leftarrow e \cdot b$.
\item[(v)] RETURN($d$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $\gcd(1326,1716)$ using GCDNoDivision.
(Show the main steps of your
work.)
\item Prove that GCDNoDivision \emph{terminates}.
\item Prove that GCDNoDivision is \emph{correct} (that is, $d=\gcd(a,b)$).\\
      You may use the following result without proof:
      $\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,b-a)$ for all integers $a$, $b$.
\item Determine the running time of GCDNoDivision in bit operations.
      The running time should be expressed in terms of $k$, where $k$
      is the bitlength of the larger of $a$ and $b$. (Justify your answer.)\\
      \underline{Hint}: Consider the binary representations of $a$ and $b$.
\item Is GCDNoDivision a polynomial-time algorithm? (Justify your answer.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
